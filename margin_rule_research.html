Margin-Style Rule Trials

Six questions about the live trading rules, each re-tested by replaying the production engine day by day over real market data. Five of the six answers say leave it alone. One question the data cannot answer, and that is recorded as unanswered rather than estimated.

Nothing changed, and that is the result

A 500-configuration grid search under 3-fold walk-forward validation found no parameter set that generalizes. The best-performing tuned configuration returns +96.7% over the full window against the untouched baseline's +155.4% — worse Sharpe, nearly double the drawdown. Every structural variant tested was neutral or harmful.

No production parameter is recommended for change. The value of this study is the five things it rules out, and the two prior claims it corrects — both of which had the sign backwards.

What was asked, and what the replay said

Every answer below is MEASURED by full engine replay at 5 bps per-side cost. No figure on this page is derived by scaling a P&L series.

Can overnight risk be reduced by closing early?
The overnight leg earns 40.9% of all held-position P&L. Forced exits at every 30-minute mark lose money; 2:30 PM CDT returns −4.9% against +38.3% for holding.
Backwards
Does checking more than once a day help?
Seven schedules from 2× to 7× daily. All lose money (−10% to −18%) against +38.3% for the single midday check, under both settlement models.
Harmful
Is the −1.51% intraday stop the right one?
Removing it entirely gives −11.6%. Tightening to −1.0% costs $14,943. Loosening to −2.5% gains $2,772 on P&L but loses on both Sharpe and drawdown.
Keep as is
Would 2:45 PM CDT specifically be better?
19:45 UTC falls inside a 30-minute bar and is not priced by the available data. The bracketing marks are shown; nothing is interpolated to reach it.
Unanswerable

Two published claims had the sign backwards

Both were produced by multiplying a frozen daily P&L series by an assumed factor. Replayed properly against real 30-minute bars, both reverse.

Checking more often

Why closing early cannot work here

A position held from one midday check to the next passes through three legs. Measuring each one separately, on 295 position-nights, explains the result above.

Where held-position P&L is actually earned

The strategy buys weakness and is paid on the bounce — which arrives overnight and in the following morning. Only the stretch after the check is negative.

Overnight gaps are genuinely violent — −$91,084 gross down against +$119,783 gross up, with a single night costing $8,187 on WDC. But an early close forgoes the up-gaps too, and the up-gaps are larger. Cutting overnight exposure cuts the edge, not the risk-adjusted loss.

Two things found along the way

A latent defect in the production engine

Guardrail 4 validates a sell with s['shares'] > pos['shares'], comparing a quantity rounded to 6 decimals against an unrounded holding. Selling a whole position therefore aborts the commit on floating-point dust — AMD 155.404388 against 155.40438799999998, off by 2×10−14. Its own mutation four lines later already tolerates exactly this.

Not live today: production trims 74.3% on a peak, never 100%, and the whole-position exits pass their share count through unrounded. It surfaced only because it blocks PEAK_SELL_PCT ≥ 1.0 from being tested at all.

Vendor history is not immutable

Re-fetching the 6,096 hourly bars underneath the reference result returned one revised bar — INTC 2026-09-04 15:00, 94.91 → 94.78. Harmless here, since the engine prices off the 17:00 bar, but a blind overwrite would have silently moved a result the project treats as ground truth. The committed bars stay authoritative on overlap; only strictly-newer bars are appended.

Reproduce any of it

Every harness refuses to emit a number until it reproduces the known $124,080.90 anchor. Data is committed under data/, never in a temporary directory.

python3 scripts/margin_style_17h_backtest.py --validate # gate: must print PASS python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py baselines # anchor + research baseline python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py costs # cost sensitivity python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py gaps # overnight decomposition python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py variants # quality filter, stops, caps python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py grid --folds 3 # 1,503 replays, ~9 min python3 scripts/margin_style_research.py stats # DSR + bootstrap verdict python3 scripts/margin_style_intraday_study.py validate # gate for the two below python3 scripts/margin_style_intraday_study.py exits python3 scripts/margin_style_intraday_study.py frequency

Scope and limits. The replay window is 132 trading days (2026-03-13 → 2026-09-21) for daily-cadence tests and 64 days (2026-06-22 → 2026-09-21) for intraday tests, bounded by how far back non-interpolated 30-minute bars reach. That sample detects large effects and would miss small ones; a marginal result here means “not detectable”, not “zero”.

All figures are MEASURED by replaying origin/main — the revision that trades real money — on $80,000 starting capital, priced at the actual 17:00 UTC bar the live trigger fires on. The engine was never modified; variants patch a copy in memory. Backtested returns describe the rules over one specific window and are not a forecast.
