Margin-Style Rule Trials

Every variation of the live rules tested so far — stops, the 1% daily stop, overnight exits, entry filters, position caps, check time — each re-tested by replaying the production engine day by day over real market data, and compared against Margin-Style Live on the same basis. The live account’s own P&L is kept separate from every backtest figure.

Nothing beats the live rules consistently

Some variants beat Margin-Style Live on one window or one price series. None beats it on every one, and none of the variants that top a column passes significance testing. Only two setups never rank worse than 4th anywhere: the live rules, and a 25% per-stock cap that beats them in just one column, by 0.5 points.

The 1% daily stop loses on every basis tested — by $45,454 over six months — and the earlier “+248%” claim for it is withdrawn. Closing out overnight positions loses too: the overnight bounce is where most of the edge is made.

The 1% daily stop

Liquidate everything and stop buying for the day once the day’s loss reaches 1% of capital. Replayed on the engine revision that carries it with its double-sell defect fixed, switched on and off by a single flag. With the flag off, that engine reproduces Margin-Style Live to the cent on all three bases, so the difference below is the stop and nothing else.

Why it loses: the stop sells into the day’s weakness, which is exactly when this strategy wants to be holding — the rebound it gives up arrives overnight and the next morning.

The earlier “+248.36% with the daily stop” is withdrawn

It came from taking the stored daily P&L of a run without the stop and capping each losing day at −$300. That assumes the stop changes nothing except the size of bad days. In reality it changes which positions exist the next morning, so the whole path diverges. Its “out-of-sample” check was a slice of the same run, and the $300 cap was 1% of $30,000 applied to an $80,000 backtest.

The stop was never on the live system — it existed only on a development branch — so no real money was exposed to it.

A timing defect in every backtest, including this one

Found while checking whether the replays price at the same moment the live system trades. They did not.

The replays priced 46 minutes after the live trigger fires

The harness prices “today” off the hourly bar labelled 17:00, using its close. Hourly bars are left-labelled — that bar spans 17:00–18:00, so its close is the 18:00 UTC (1:00 PM CDT) price. The live trigger fires at ~17:14 UTC (12:14 PM CDT). Verified against 30-minute bars, not inferred from the label: over 24 symbol-days the hourly close matches the 30-minute 17:30 bar 23 times.

The published +155.10% is not withdrawn. It is the exact, reproducible output of its stated method — just priced at about 1:00 PM CDT, on hourly bars. What the same rules would have returned over six months at the live check time is unknown — see below.

…and the hourly series itself is wrong at about 7% of instants

Checked against 1-minute prints, the hourly and 30-minute series disagree on about 7% of prices at the same nominal time, and the 30-minute series is right 18 times out of 21. Because the engine trades on 0.4% and −1.51% thresholds, a tiny price difference flips a rule and the whole path diverges: on the same 64 days and the same instant, hourly bars give $17,811 and 30-minute bars $28,131.

So the “+125.9% live-accurate” figure and the “production ranks first outright” table previously shown here are withdrawn — both were priced on hourly bars. An accurate six-month figure at the live check time cannot be produced: real 30-minute bars start on 22 June.

Why closing early cannot work here

A position held from one midday check to the next passes through three legs. Measuring each one separately, on 295 position-nights, explains the result above.

Where held-position P&L is actually earned

The strategy buys weakness and is paid on the bounce — which arrives overnight and in the following morning. Only the stretch after the check is negative.

Overnight gaps are genuinely violent — −$91,084 gross down against +$119,783 gross up, with a single night costing $8,187 on WDC. But an early close forgoes the up-gaps too, and the up-gaps are larger. Cutting overnight exposure cuts the edge, not the risk-adjusted loss.

Gap reduction: how much risk comes off, and what it costs

The earlier exits above leave early, giving up the last of the session and the overnight gap. This isolates the gap alone: sell at the closing bell (20:00 UTC), keeping the whole session, and hold nothing overnight. Partial exits make the trade-off a curve instead of two endpoints.

Two things only this test could show. First, removing only the gap — keeping every minute of the session — still turns +38.3% into −4.8%. The gap is not incidental to the edge; it is the edge. Second, drawdown improves all the way to 75% removal (−12.4% → −5.9%) and then gets worse at 100% (−14.9%) — with no overnight exposure the system loses steadily and grinds a fresh drawdown out of a falling equity curve. Full elimination is worse than partial on both axes.

On the earlier “100% gap reduction by closing at 2:45 PM”

The risk half was right. Hold nothing overnight and you carry exactly zero overnight gap exposure — 100% removal, by construction. Measured here, annualized volatility falls 61.3% → 16.7%.

The return half was backwards. That setup was presented as roughly +100%. Replayed, closing at the bell returns −4.8% and closing at 2:30 PM CDT returns −4.94%. The figure came from multiplying a stored daily-P&L series by an assumed factor, which cannot model an exit rule because closing early changes which positions exist the next morning.

Two things found along the way

A latent defect in the production engine

Guardrail 4 validates a sell with s['shares'] > pos['shares'], comparing a quantity rounded to 6 decimals against an unrounded holding. Selling a whole position therefore aborts the commit on floating-point dust — AMD 155.404388 against 155.40438799999998, off by 2×10−14. Its own mutation four lines later already tolerates exactly this.

Not live today: production trims 74.3% on a peak, never 100%, and the whole-position exits pass their share count through unrounded. It surfaced only because it blocks PEAK_SELL_PCT ≥ 1.0 from being tested at all.

Vendor history is not immutable

Re-fetching the 6,096 hourly bars underneath the reference result returned one revised bar — INTC 2026-09-04 15:00, 94.91 → 94.78. Harmless here, since the engine prices off the 17:00 bar, but a blind overwrite would have silently moved a result the project treats as ground truth. The committed bars stay authoritative on overlap; only strictly-newer bars are appended.

Reproduce any of it

Every harness refuses to emit a number until it reproduces the known $124,080.90 anchor. Data is committed under data/, never in a temporary directory.

Scope and limits. The replay window is 132 trading days (2026-03-13 → 2026-09-21) for daily-cadence tests and 64 days (2026-06-22 → 2026-09-21) for intraday tests, bounded by how far back non-interpolated 30-minute bars reach. That sample detects large effects and would miss small ones; a marginal result here means “not detectable”, not “zero”.
