    How to use it, and what it cannot do

    The idea. While a stock is falling, each bounce stops below the previous bounce's high and then a new low follows. The picture changes only when a bounce holds above the last low and then trades above the previous bounce's high. That price is the level to beat. Buying below it is guessing the bottom. Buying above it means the pattern of lower highs has broken, and today's low becomes the price that proves you wrong.

    The limits. These are standard tape-reading rules of thumb. They have not been tested on this page's data, and earlier replays in this project found no reliable edge in day-trading these stocks. A turn that passes every check can still fail, and it often does on days with bad news. The checks tell you the selling has paused. They do not tell you it is over.

    What it reads. Only price quotes and minute bars for the stock you pick. It never reads an account and cannot place an order. Minute bars arrive when each minute completes, and the page refreshes about every 30 seconds while it is open and visible, so it is a few seconds to a minute behind the tape.
